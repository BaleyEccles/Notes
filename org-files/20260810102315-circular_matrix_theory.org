* Circular Matrix Theory
\[C = \begin{bmatrix}
c_0 & c_{n-1}& c_{n-2} & \hdots & c_1 \\
c_1 & c_0 & c_{n-1}& \hdots & c_2 \\
c_2 & c_1 & c_0 & \hdots & c_3 \\
\vdots & c_2 & c_1  & \hdots & \vdots \\
c_{n-2} & \vdots & c_2 & \hdots & c_{N-1} \\
c_{n-1} & c_{n-2} & \vdots & \hdots & c_0
\end{bmatrix}\] 

Can form a circular matrix $C$ if only a vector $c = [c_0\ c_1\ \hdots\ c_{N-1}]$ is defined
 - The [[id:e7ad3ee3-7394-40ed-b2a3-ca0815bd9280][Eigenvalues]] of $c$ are the column of the [[id:38634596-7d68-46dc-a12c-bb0d244f7835][DFT]] matrix of the same size
 - We would calculate these as $cF$
This means we can find:
\[C = \frac{1}{N}F^H\Lambda F\]
If we define $\lambda = [\lambda_0\ \lambda_1\ \hdots\ \lambda_{n-1}]$, then $\lambda = cF$
Also it will be useful to normalise the DFT: $\mathcal{F} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}F$, so $\mathcal{F}\mathcal{F}^H = I$
