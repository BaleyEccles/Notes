* Equalisation
\[Z_{in}(t)\rightarrow \boxed{H_t(f)} (S_{in}(t)) \rightarrow \boxed{H_c(f)}\rightarrow \boxed{+n(t)} (y(t)) \rightarrow \boxed{\textrm{Equaliser}} (S_{out}(t)) \rightarrow \boxed{H_r(f)}\rightarrow Z_{out}(t)\]
 - Equaliser and $H_r(f)$ may be combined
 - Assume [[id:d535e784-50d7-4f0c-9188-5f42e3aad514][Intersymbol Interference (ISI)]] only lasts for a finite amount of time
 - The total number of samples resulting in [[id:7ea297c6-78cb-4a6b-b4a5-b0af9176122e][dispersion]] is $L$

Naively, we want: $H_{eq}(f) = \frac{1}{H_c(f)}$
 - This will cause infinite gains and amplify noise
\begin{align*}
S_{out}(f) &= [\sin(f)H_c(f) + N(f)]H_{eq}(f) \\
S_{out}(f) &= \sin(f) + N(f)H_{eq}(f) \\
N(f)H_{eq}(f) &= N'(f)
\end{align*}
$N'(f)$ is coloured Gaussian noise, with $S_{N'N'}(f) = \frac{0.5 N_0}{|H_c(f)|^2}$, which risks significant noise enhancement. The SNR drops dramatically with this filter.

\[Z_{in}(t)\rightarrow \boxed{h_t(t)} \rightarrow \boxed{h_c(t)} \rightarrow \boxed{+ n(t)}(w(t)) \rightarrow \boxed{h_r(t) = g_m^{*}(-t)}(y(k)) \rightarrow \boxed{\textrm{Sampler}} \rightarrow (y[n]) \rightarrow \boxed{\textrm{Adaptive Equaliser}} \leftrightarrow (\textrm{output})\]
 - $h(t) = h_t(t)*h_c(t)$
 - $Z_{in}(t)$ with input symbols $a[k]$
 - Adaptive Equaliser takes signals from the output and updates the weights in the equaliser using a Tap Width Algorithm.
\begin{align*}
Z_{in}(t) &= \sum_k a[k]\delta(t - kT_s) \\
w(t) &= h_t(t) * h_c(t) * Z_{in}(t) + n(t) \\
\end{align*}
We want $h_r(t) = h_c(-t) * h_t(-t)$ to minimise SNR, but this is difficult if $h_c(t) \neq \delta(t)$ and is time-varying.
Define $f(t) = h_t(t) * h_c(t) * h_r(t)$, so
\begin{align*}
y(t) &= Z_{in}(t) * f(t) + n(t)*h_r(t) \\
&= \sum_k a[k] f(t - kT_s) + n_g(t)
\end{align*}
Let $f[n] = f(nT_s)$ denote samples of $f(t)$ at $nT_s$, so
\begin{align*}
y[n] &= \sum_{k = -\infty}^{\infty}a[k]f[n - k] + n_g[n] \\
&= a[n]f[0] + \sum_{k\neq n} a[k]f[n - k] + n_g[n]
\end{align*}
 - $a[n]f[0]$ is the desired data symbol
 - $\sum_{k\neq n} a[k]f[n - k]$ is the [[id:d535e784-50d7-4f0c-9188-5f42e3aad514][Intersymbol Interference (ISI)]]
 - $n_g[n]$ is the samples noise
   
We get the [[id:d535e784-50d7-4f0c-9188-5f42e3aad514][ISI]] free condition iff:
\[F_{\Sigma}(f) = \frac{1}{T_s}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}F\left(f + \frac{n}{T_s}\right) = f[0]\]
 - $F_{\Sigma}(f)$ is the Folded Spectrum
If $F_{\Sigma}(f)$ is not flat, we use $H_{eq}(t)$ to correct it.

*** Zero-Forcing (ZF) Equaliser
 - Minimise Peak Distortion
ZF cancel [[id:d535e784-50d7-4f0c-9188-5f42e3aad514][Intersymbol Interference (ISI)]] but risks significant noise enhancement.

Noise Power:
\[N(z) = \frac{N_0}{|H(z)|^2}\]
Normalised Peak Distortion:
\[D_0 = \frac{1}{|\hat{y}[0]|}\sum_{k\neq 0}|\hat{y}[k]|\]
Where:
\[\hat{y}[n] = \begin{cases}
1 &, n = 0 \\
0 &, n \neq 0
\end{cases}\]
Note $\hat{y}[n]$ is only able to be applied for $n\in [-k, k]$. So, the zero forcing condition becomes:
\[\begin{cases}
\hat{y}[n] = \sum_{j = -k}^k c_jy[n - j] \\
\hat{y}[n] = \begin{cases}
1 &, n = 0 \\
0 &, n = \pm 1, \pm 2, \hdots, \pm k
\end{cases}
\end{cases}\]
Which can also be written as:
\begin{align*}
\begin{bmatrix}
y[0] & y[-1] & \hdots & y[-2k] \\
y[1] & y[0] & \hdots & y[-2k+1] \\
\vdots & \vdots & \hdots & \vdots \\
y[2k] & y[2k-1] & \hdots & y[0] \\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
c_{-k} \\
c_{-k +1} \\
\vdots \\
c_{0} \\
\vdots \\
c_{k-1} \\
c_{k}
\end{bmatrix} &= \begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
\vdots \\
0 \\
1 \\
0 \\
\vdots \\
0 \\
\end{bmatrix} \\
YC &= \hat{Y} \\
C &= Y^{-1}\hat{Y}
\end{align*}

**** Tolerance of ZF equaliser to noise
\[\sigma_{\hat{y}}^2 = \sigma^2\sum_{j=-k}^k|c_j|^2\]

*** Minimum Mean Square Error Equaliser (MMSE)
 - Minimise the mean squared error
\[\{c_k\} = \min_{c_k}\{E[(a[k] - \hat{a}[k])^2]\}\]
\[\hat{a}[k]= \sum_{j = -L}^Lc_j y[k - j]\]

Made up of a noise whitening component and a [[id:d535e784-50d7-4f0c-9188-5f42e3aad514][Intersymbol Interference (ISI)]] removal component
\[H_{eq}(z) = \frac{1}{F(z) + N_0}\]
Three important features:
1. The ideal infinite-length MMSE equaliser cancels the noise whitening filter
2. The infinite-length equaliser is identical to the ZF equaliser except for the noise term $N_0$
3. There is a balance between inverting the channel and noise
\[C = Y^H[Y^HY + N_0I]^{-1}\hat{Y}\]
 - $H$ indicates the [[id:51260ef9-3b99-4fa4-a4f2-ff241ff32bff][Hermitian Transpose]]

**** Performance
\[J_{\min} = T_s\int_{-\frac{1}{2T_s}}^{\frac{1}{2T_s}}\frac{N_0}{F_{\Sigma}(f) + N_0} df\]
Has the properties:
 - $0\leq J_{\min} = E\{[a[k] - \hat{a}[k]]^2\} \leq 1$
 - $J_{\min} = 0$ when $N_0 = 0$ as long as $F_{\Sigma}(f) \neq 0$ within the signal bandwidth
 - $J_{\min} = 1$ if $N_0 \rightarrow \infty$

** Nonlinear Equalisers
*** Decision Feedback Detector/Equaliser (DFD/DFE)
Incoming signal set $x = [x_1, x_2, \hdots, x_N]^T$
 - Gaussian distributed
\[f(x_1, x_2, \hdots, x_N) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^N\det(\Sigma)}}e^{-\frac{x^T\Sigma x}{2}}\]
Where $\Sigma$ is the autocovariance matrix of $x$
\begin{align*}
\textrm{cov}(X, Y) &= E\{(X - E(X))\overline{(Y - E(Y))}\} \\
&= E\{X\overline{Y}\} - E\{X\}E\{\overline{Y}\} \\
\Sigma &=\begin{bmatrix}
\textrm{cov}(x_1, x_1) & \textrm{cov}(x_1, x_2) & \hdots & \textrm{cov}(x_1, x_N) \\
\textrm{cov}(x_2, x_1) & \textrm{cov}(x_2, x_2) & \hdots & \textrm{cov}(x_2, x_N) \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
\textrm{cov}(x_N, x_1) & \textrm{cov}(x_N, x_2) & \hdots & \textrm{cov}(x_N, x_N) \\
\end{bmatrix}
\end{align*}
Use $\Delta^2 = x^T\Sigma^{-1}x$ as a sufficient probability metric.

If $x$ is and i.i.d sequence, $\textrm{cov}(x_i,x_j) = 0$ whenever $i \neq j$ and $\textrm{cov}(x_i, x_i) = 1$. This means $\Sigma = I \Rightarrow \Sigma^{-1} = I$
\[\Rightarrow \Delta^2 = x^Tx = |x|^2 = \sum_{i=1}^N x_i^2\]
So,
\begin{align*}
f(x_1, x_2, \hdots, x_N) &= \frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^N\det(\Sigma)}}e^{-\frac{x^T\sum_{i=1}^N x_i^2}{2}} \\
&= \frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^N\det(\Sigma)}}\prod_{i=1}^Ne^{-\frac{x_i^2}{2}}
\end{align*}
DFD attempts to maximise the $i^{th}$ factor in the product when detecting the $i^{th}$ signal.
For symbols $a[1], a[2],\hdots, a[i],\hdots, a[N]$, we designate estimates of these as $\hat{a}[1], \hat{a}[2],\hdots, \hat{a}[i],\hdots, \hat{a}[N]$. When detecting symbol $i$, assume we already have estimates $\hat{a}[i-1], \hdots, \hat{a}[2], \hat{a}[1]$. So, the probability distribution for symbol $a[i]$ is:
\[\Pr(a[i]|[r[i],\underline{\hat{a}}]) = P_0 e^{-\frac{(r[i] - h[0]a[i] - \sum_{k=1}^Mh[k]\hat{a}[i-k])^2}{\sigma^2}}\]
 - $\underline{\hat{a}} = (\hat{a}[1], \hat{a}[2], \hdots ,\hat{a}[i-1])$
\[r[i] - h[0]a[i] - \sum_{k=1}^Mh[k]\hat{a}[i-k]\]
 - This is just trying to get the noise term

\[DFD \Rightarrow \min_{a[i]}\left(r[i] - h[0]a[i] - \sum_{k=1}^Mh[k]\hat{a}[i - k]\right)\]
 - $h[n]$ is the channel impulse response (found by [[id:533199e6-1d41-4223-a95e-b4ee0de5f926][Pilot Symbols]])
 - $\hat{a}[n]$ previously-determined sequence of inputs estimated at the receiver
 - $r[i]$ current received values
 - $a[i]$ current set of possible choices in the constellation
   
** Maximum Aposteriori Probability (MAP) Detector
The problem: given a set of measured statistics $r[i]$, what is the most likely set of initial symbols that produced this evidence?
Using Bayesian terms:
\[\Pr(a[i]|r[i]) = \frac{\Pr(r[i]|a[i])\Pr(a[i])}{\Pr(r[i])}\]
Find $$\max_{a[i]}\Pr(a[i]|r[i])$$
This comes from Baye's Rule $\Pr(X|Y) = \frac{\Pr(Y|X)\Pr(X)}{\Pr(Y)}$
 - $\Pr(X)$ is the 'prior probability'
 - $\Pr(Y|X)$ is the 'likelihood function' - probability of seeing evidence $y$ given parameter $x$
 - $\Pr(Y|X)$ is the 'posterior probability' - probability of seeing evidence $x$ given parameter $y$
   
Hidden Markov Model:
In a graph of an initial known state ($X_0$) and final known state ($X_N$) and unknown state between ($X_k = \{X_1, X_2, \hdots X_{N-1}\}$). For each unknowns state we get and evidence $E_k$, we want to maximise all these evidences. $E_k$ only depends on $X_k$. Prior probability: $\Pr(E_k = e_k|X_k)$, and $e_k$ comes from our measurement of $r[k]$, at time $k$. Probability of hidden variable is inferred from:
\[\Pr(X_k|[E_1 = e_1, \hdots, E_N = e_N])\]
We want to find $$x_k = \max_{x_k}(\Pr(X_k = x_k| [e_1, \hdots, e_N]))$$
Our approach is iterative and comprises:
 - forward message (move from time $0\rightarrow k$), allowing probability distribution of $X_k$ to be determined
 - Backward message (mores from time $N+1 \rightarrow k$), allows update to produce posterior probability
   
Let $e_{1:k} = \{e_1, e_2, \hdots, e_k\}$ and $e_{k+1:N} = \{e_{k+1}, e_{k+2},\hdots, e_N\}$. So we can rewrite it as:
\begin{align*}
x_k &= \max_{x_k}(\Pr(X_k=x_k|[e_{1:k}, e_{k+1:N}])) \\
&= \max_{x_k}\left(\frac{\Pr(e_{k+1:N}|[X_k=x_k, e_{1:k}]) \Pr(X_k=x_k|e_{1:k})}{\Pr(e_k)}\right) \\
&= \max_{x_k}\left(\Pr(e_{k+1:N}|[X_k=x_k, e_{1:k}]) \Pr(X_k=x_k|e_{1:k})\right) \\
&= \max_{x_k}\left(\Pr(e_{k+1:N}|X_k=x_k) \otimes \Pr(X_k=x_k|e_{1:k})\right) \\
\end{align*}
 - $\otimes$ is the piece wise multiplication
_The Forward Message_
Itterativley , we want to find the next part of $\Pr(X_{k+1}|e_{1:k+1})$ from $\Pr(X_k|e_{1:k})$. Since the state is a column vector with $M$ symbols, the message is also a column vector with $M$ symbols.
At step $k+1$, we receive $E_{k+1}$, and this revises  the likelihood function: $\Pr(E_{k+1}= e_{k+1}|X_{k+1})$
From Baye's Rule: $\Pr(X_{k+1}|[e_{1:k},e_{k+1}]) = \beta\Pr(e_{k+1}|[X_{k+1}e_{1:k}])\Pr(X_{k+1}|e_{1:k})$
Using _Probability marginalisation:_ $\Pr(A) = \sum_b\Pr(A, B=b)$
\[\Rightarrow \Pr(X_{k+1}|[e_{1:k},e_{k+1}]) = \beta_1 \sum_{x_k}\Pr(e_{k+1}|x_{k+1})\Pr(X_{k+1}, x_k|e_{1:k})\]
Using _General probability multiplication:_ $\Pr(A) = \sum_b\Pr(A, B=b) = \sum_b\Pr(A|B=b)\Pr(B=b)$
\[\Rightarrow \boxed{\Pr(X_{k+1}|[e_{1:k},e_{k+1}]) = \beta_1 \Pr(e_{k+1}|X_{k+1})\sum_{x_k}\Pr(X_{k+1}|[x_ke_{1:k}])\Pr(x_k|e_{1:k})}\]
 - Forward message at $k=1$
   - $\Pr(X_{k+1}|[e_{1:k},e_{k+1}])$
 - New evidence
   - $\Pr(e_{k+1}|X_{k+1})$
 - State transition probability
   - $\Pr(X_{k+1}|[x_ke_{1:k}])$
 - Forward message at $k$
   - $\Pr(x_k|e_{1:k})$
     
_Backwards Message:_
\[\Pr(e_{k+1:N|X_k}) = \beta_2\sum_{x_{k+1}}\Pr(e_{k+1}|x_{k+1})\Pr(e_{k+2:N}|x_{k+1})\Pr(x_{k+1}|X_k)\]
 - Backwards message at $k+1$
   - $\Pr(e_{k+1:N|X_k})$
 - New evidence at $k+1$
   - $\Pr(e_{k+1}|x_{k+1})$
 - Backwards message at $k+2$
   - $\Pr(e_{k+2:N}|x_{k+1})$
 - State transition probability
   - $\Pr(x_{k+1}|X_k)$


*** Matrix implementation of forward-backwards messages
asssume $M$ levels of modulation so $X_k = \{x_1, \hdots, x_M\}$. We implement
1. The state transition model, $\Pr(X_k|X_{k-1})$, given by:
\[T = \begin{bmatrix}
\Pr(x_k = x_1 | x_{k-1} = x_1) & \Pr(x_k = x_2 | x_{k-1} = x_1) & \hdots & \Pr(x_k = x_M | x_{k-1} = x_1) \\
\Pr(x_k = x_1 | x_{k-1} = x_2) & \Pr(x_k = x_2 | x_{k-1} = x_2) & \hdots & \Pr(x_k = x_M | x_{k-1} = x_2) \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
\Pr(x_k = x_1 | x_{k-1} = x_M) & \Pr(x_k = x_2 | x_{k-1} = x_M) & \hdots & \Pr(x_k = x_M | x_{k-1} = x_M)
\end{bmatrix}\]
2. The observation matrix $O_k$ from $\Pr(e_k|X_k)$
\[O_k = \begin{bmatrix}
\Pr(e_k|x_k=x_1) & 0 & \hdots & 0 \\
0 & \Pr(e_k|x_k=x_2) & \hdots & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \hdots & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \hdots & \Pr(e_k|x_k=x_M)
\end{bmatrix}\]
3. Let  the forward message be:
\[z^k = [z_1^k\ z_2^k\ \hdots z_M^k]^T\]
 - $k$ camto iterations
Then, $z^{k+1} = O_{k+1}T^Tz^k$
4. Let the backwards message be:
\[b^k = [b_1^k\ b_2^k\ \hdots b_M^k]^T\]
\[b^{k+1} = T O_{k+1}b^{k+2}\]
5. MAP distributions:
\begin{align*}
\max\{\Pr(X_k|e_{1:N})\} &= \max_{x_k}\{z^k\otimes b^{k+1}\} \\
&= \begin{bmatrix}
z_1^kb_1^{k+1} \\
z_2^kb_2^{k+1} \\
\vdots \\
z_M^kb_M^{k+1}
\end{bmatrix}
\end{align*}
